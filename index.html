So, what is PyDelhi?

A volunteer-run community, since 2011.

Students, professionals, teachers and founders across Delhi NCR who share one thing: they like Python, and they like learning from each other.

  • 15years of Saturdays
  • 14k+members
  • 350+speakers on our stages

What we do

More than a monthly meetup.

  • Talk meetups

    The regular Saturday: talks, lightning talks and conversation over chai.

  • Workshops

    Hands-on sessions where you code along, at meetups and at colleges.

  • Birds of a Feather

    Small circles around one topic, where everyone gets a turn.

  • Dev sprints

    A day of open source, with mentors beside first-time contributors.

  • Joint meetups

    Mega meetups with other communities in the city.

  • Conferences

    PyDelhi Conf, and a hand in bringing PyCon India to Delhi.

Our story

Fifteen years, one Saturday at a time.

  1. 2011The first meetups

    A few Python developers start meeting. Their mailing list, ncr-python.in, still runs.

  2. 2016PyDelhi Conf

    The first conference, and PyCon India comes to New Delhi.

  3. 2018Mega meetups

    International Women's Day, and a FOSS meetup at Adobe with communities from across the city.

  4. 2019Dev sprints

    First-timers send their first open source pull requests.

  5. 2023Conf returns

    A third edition, with talks recorded on YouTube.

  6. NowEvery month

    Meetups and RSVPs on Commudle, proposals reviewed in the open on GitHub.

What a meetup is like

Show up. Stay for the chai.

How it helps you

What's in it for you?

  • Hire or get hired

    Say who you're hiring, or what you're looking for, right on stage.

  • Find collaborators

    Meet people who want to build the thing you're building.

  • Learn new stuff

    Real talks from people who shipped it, and time to ask.

  • Get mentored

    Someone a few steps ahead is always around for advice.

  • Make friends

    Like-minded, slightly geeky, and good company.

Take the stage

Everyone has something to share.

Most of the room is new to Python or to speaking. Pick a length that suits you.

  • Lightning talk5 min

    One idea, slides optional. The easiest way onto the stage.

  • Talk20 to 30 min

    Something you built, broke or learned, with time for questions.

  • Workshop60 to 90 min

    Hands-on, where the room codes along with you.

Proposals are reviewed in the open → github.com/pydelhi/talks

How you can help

Meetups run on people.

  • Host us

    A venue for a Saturday afternoon: a hall, a projector, Wi-Fi.

  • Sponsor

    Food, chai, recordings and swag. Every meetup stays free.

  • Volunteer

    Review talks, run the room, write the recap. Most of us started by helping once.

  • Spread the word

    Bring a friend, share an event, tell your college.

Want to code? Projects live at github.com/pydelhi
